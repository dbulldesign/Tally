## Design Specificity Verdict

The work surface is authored for this shop and the shell is interchangeable — and the shell wins the screen. At 760px the interchangeable furniture (titlebar + toolbar + rail + inspector + total bar) takes 43% of the window; the worksheet gets 434 of 760px. The titlebar's only interactive slot is a theme switch, duplicated in two other places.

Authored, and good: the QTY/EXPRESSION/RESULT grid; one amber tabular-nums voice for every measured quantity; the pin dot composing into a self-describing total; sticky section bands carrying `11′ 10.5″ of 28′ 0.5″`; NOT IN TOTAL; and the whole Cut tab, whose `FIXTURE ZONES → CHANNEL` and `↔ CH1 · 8′ 0″` are domain sentences rather than chart titles.

The surface still undercuts itself where it counts: it renders `10.32″` and `17.292828 m` to a shop that cuts sixteenths, while the engine parses `4' 6 1/2"` and refuses to speak it back. Settings offers five panel toggles and no fraction, precision, or rounding setting — chrome configurable, domain not.

Deleting Project Map was right and it is genuinely gone. Committing to the colour rail is not the win it looked like: six fixed colours assigned `pal[length % 6]`, so worksheet 7 wears worksheet 1's colour, at exactly the widths (760–1099) where colour is the only identifier — and the rail discards the per-worksheet line count the docked sidebar shows. One generic panel traded for one generic rail, minus data. The context-menu swatches are a real improvement.

## Overall Impression

The number the app funnels into is now trustworthy and says so — that was the point of the last pass and it worked. What the re-review exposes is that the honesty machinery stops at the door of the highest-stakes screen: the Cut tab ignores pinning and the tag filter, folds `@LA.A`/`@LA.B` into `@LA`, and renders a 120″ piece against 96″ stock as `96″ / 96″` with a full green bar. That is a purchase order for the wrong material, endorsed in green. Meanwhile a mistyped quantity hangs the app beyond recovery. Both predate this work; both outrank everything cosmetic.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] A quantity typed into QTY hangs the app permanently.** `collectPieces()` expands the QTY field into that many objects before `pack()` sorts them, so `12"` with QTY `99999999` allocates ~10^8 objects. Verified through the real UI: the tab never renders again, and if the value reaches localStorage the app never boots. There is no reset in the UI. A paste from Excel or a mis-keyed 12 → 1200000 bricks an estimator's only copy of the day. Fix: clamp QTY at parse with a visible "capped at 9999" note, cap total pieces in collectPieces and report truncation, and reject non-numeric instead of coercing to 1. → /impeccable harden

**[P0] The cut plan reports impossible cuts as fitting exactly.** `pack()` sets `over:true` for a piece longer than stock; the template never reads it, so `used = stock`, `pct = 100`, colour falls through to --good. Verified: 120" and 98" pieces against 96" stock both render `96″ / 96″` with full green bars and a `20″ drop`. This is the screen a buyer orders from. Fix: render the over flag — red bar, "piece is 120″, longer than 96″ stock", exclude from drop, and a per-plan banner. → /impeccable harden

**[P1] My multi-window fix is incomplete, and the bar it added invites the loss.** The writer counter is written and never read — `onForeignWrite` compares only the writer id — and `pagehide → flushSave()` writes unconditionally without comparing what is in storage. Verified end to end: reloading the stale tab still destroyed the other window's lines. The bar also fires in both windows on the first keystroke and "Keep mine" does not clear it, and it says "this worksheet" while the payload covers every worksheet, fixture and channel. Since refreshing is the natural response to an undismissable warning, the warning now leads to the data loss it was added to prevent. Fix: read the counter on flush and refuse to overwrite a newer one; clear the bar when the incoming payload matches local state; only raise it when local is genuinely dirty; label both buttons with what dies and add "Keep both — open theirs as a new worksheet"; keep a rolling snapshot under a second key. → /impeccable harden

**[P1] Two different answers for @LA, and the Cut tab ignores pinning and the filter.** `inView()` matches tags exactly, so filtering `@LA` yields 22′ 6.32″; `splitTag()` folds `LA.A`/`LA.B` into `LA` as zones, so the Cut tab reports 40′ 4.82″ for the same fixture — a 214″ difference — while the sidebar lists `@LA`, `@LA.A`, `@LA.B` as three unrelated tags. `collectPieces()` reads neither `pinned` nor the filter, so unpinned lines are in the plan. This is last run's P0 one level up: two adjacent tools give different quantities for the same tag. Fix: make `@LA` match `LA` and all `LA.*` with zones nested in the sidebar; put a scope line at the top of the Cut tab, or honour pin/filter with an explicit "include unpinned" toggle. → /impeccable clarify

**[P1] The total bar: unreadable figures in light mode and broken geometry on real phones — three of them mine.** The breakdown chip figures are --amber #9c5a0d on the chip's translucent white over --total = **2.30:1**; darkening light-mode amber fixed body text and broke the numbers on the dark bar. Dark Σ when a section is fully pinned is #fff on #e6a444 = **2.15:1**. Cut-tab `20″ drop` / `7′ 7.18″ left` is --warn on white = **2.06:1**. `totalSub` still prints all three alt units at six decimals at every width — the phone fix covered rows only. At 360px and 320px the total number and alt-units line hard-overlap the breakdown chips; at 375px the label wraps to three lines. Chips are sorted by magnitude, so the section you are working in is the one hidden behind "+3 more". Fix: a dedicated on-dark amber for the total bar, a darker --warn for text, hide totalSub under 760px, let the number block own its row under 420px, cap the label to one line, order chips in sheet order. → /impeccable adapt
