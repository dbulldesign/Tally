## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment (unanchored).** Split verdict, and the split is instructive: the data model and the Cut tab are unmistakably authored for a linear-lighting estimator, while the shell around them is a generic productivity app. Fixtures and Channels as separate first-class entities, each with stock length and cut increment plus a fixture→channel link, encode the actual physics of the trade — an extrusion and its insert are ordered together and cut to different increments. `FIXTURE ZONES → CHANNEL` with per-stick utilisation bars is a purchasing document, not a calculator readout. Two-level tags (`@LA.North`) match how a takeoff is organised by elevation. The phone key row surfacing `' " # @` is something only someone who has typed measurements on a phone would build.

Against that: the Quick Calc placeholder reads `20% off $19  or  8*33.79"` — a retail-discount example in a lighting worksheet, the clearest tell that the shell came from a generic-calculator template. `Yd`/`m`/`cm` hold three of seven chips in the most prominent control in a US architectural shop, and `5.172075 m` is forced onto every row and every printed line. A faux macOS title bar spends 86px of vertical chrome inside a browser tab that already has its own, in a view that shows nine rows. The version badge occupies the brand slot. Five "turn any panel off" toggles offer configurability where a point of view belongs.

The specificity lives in the least prominent quarter of the screen; the generic apparatus owns the top 86px and the primary toolbar.

**Deterministic scan.** The bundled CLI detector returns **exit 0, `[]`** for both `index.html` and the extracted template — genuinely clean, but a weak signal: the static engine has no layout engine, and the whole app is a JSON string on one line. ## Overall Impression

The numbers inside this app are now trustworthy — that is a real achievement over the last ten versions, and it is where the effort went. What has not been examined is the artifact the app exists to produce. Every session ends at the printer, and the printed sheet is the worst screen in the product: the Cut plan — the one page a foreman actually needs — is `display:none` in the print block at every width, confirmed by measurement; pin radios and Σ buttons print as page furniture at 44×44; there is no job number, no revision, no date, no page numbers and no repeated column header. Peak-end says Tally will be remembered by that sheet.

The single biggest opportunity is not a feature. It is that three of the four numbers on every row are noise nobody asked for, they are why only nine rows fit on a 900px screen, they follow the sheet onto paper, and the seven-chip control that appears to govern them has two identical options and cannot remove any of them.

## What's Working

**The unitless-result guard.** A row whose expression yields a dimensionless number is badged `NOT IN TOTAL` in amber and excluded, and the total bar reports `· 5 NOT COUNTED`. The bug class that destroys trust in a calculator is the silently-wrong sum; this makes exclusion visible at both the row and the aggregate. Someone designed for the failure mode instead of the happy path.

**The collapsed section rollup.** `▶ LOBBY — COVE, EAST + WEST · 4 lines · Σ 61′ 8 1/4″ of 106′ 1 9/16″` makes the *structure* of the takeoff the interface. An estimator thinks in areas and elevations, not rows, and this is the only view that matches that model — and the only place the app is genuinely dense.

**The domain model exposed as UI.** Fixtures with MAX/INC, channels as a separate cut stream, an explicit link between them, two-level tags building the sidebar tree, and the Cut tab's per-stick bars as the payoff. It answers a purchasing question, not just an arithmetic one.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] The printed sheet cannot do the job it exists for**
- **Why it matters.** This is the artifact that leaves the office and drives a purchase order. B confirmed `.tinspector` is `display:none` in the print block, so the Cut plan can never be printed at any width; `.tcell-pin` and `.tsum` both render visible at 44×44 on paper as dead furniture. There is no job number, revision, date, `Page N of M`, or repeated column header — on worksheets whose siblings are named "Addendum 3 — revised cove". The in-progress empty line prints its placeholder (`e.g. 4' 6"  #Rail  @LA`) as a line item.
- **Fix.** Add `.tcell-pin, .tsum { display: none !important }` to the print block. Suppress rows with an empty expression. Add a print-only header carrying worksheet name, date and revision, repeat the column header per page, and number pages. Give the Cut plan its own printed page instead of hiding it.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`
- *Note on a disputed detail:* A reported that section subtotals compute to 0×0 in print, printing `0′ 0″` as the only number. B measured `.tsecsub` at **37.6×15 — visible** — and found only `.tsecalt` at 0×0 via `display:none`, which is deliberate. A's mechanism is wrong; the printed section number is present. I am not carrying that claim forward.

**[P0] The running total contradicts itself under a filter**
- **Why it matters.** Filter to a tag while a line outside it is pinned, and the bar named `RUNNING TOTAL` shows `—` with copy claiming nothing is pinned. The pin state is intact; the region the user trusts for the number is asserting the opposite. The header simultaneously still reports the unfiltered line count.
- **Fix.** Never show the empty state while pinned lines exist — say `1 pinned line hidden by @CH2 · 61′ 8 1/4″`. Show the worksheet total as a persistent second figure so the bar is never blank. Change the header to `2 of 18 lines` under any filter.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

**[P1] Three of the four numbers on every row cannot be turned off**
- **Why it matters.** Every row prints `16.96875 ft`, `203 5/8″` and `5.172075 m` beneath its result in a US architectural shop. They are why only nine rows fit at 1280×900, Compact buys exactly one more, and they follow the sheet to paper. The control that appears to govern them — seven chips holding seven of the first nine tab stops — has two options (`Auto`, `Ft+In`) that B confirmed produce byte-identical output, and none that remove anything.
- **Fix.** Three chips: `Ft + In` / `Inches` / `Decimal ft`, and make the choice *replace* rather than reorder. Move metric behind one "also show metric" setting, default off. Reclaim the space for the worksheet total.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

**[P1] Feet-dash-inches, as drawn on the drawings, silently becomes subtraction**
- **Why it matters.** `8-5 13/16` → `2.1875`. `4-6 1/2` → `-2.5`, a negative length, displayed. A transcription straight off a drawing produces a plausible small number with an amber badge that says only that it isn't counted — never that the notation was reinterpreted.
- **Fix.** Detect `<int>-<int>( <frac>)?` and either parse it as feet-dash-inches or ask: *"did you mean 8′ 5 13/16″?"*. Flag a negative length as an error in its own right.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] Nothing the user chooses is remembered, and 23 tab stops precede the first field**
- **Why it matters.** Theme, density and format are absent from the persisted document: Dark → reload → Light, Compact → reload → Comfy, every morning, and the theme is settable in three places none of which stick. Meanwhile B counted 23 tab stops before the first expression field, because all chrome precedes the worksheet in document order — in an app whose stated success metric is speed of capture.
- **Fix.** Add `theme`, `density`, `format` to the persisted document; seed theme from `prefers-color-scheme` on first run only; delete two of the three theme controls. Autofocus row 1 on load, and take the format chips out of the tab order (or move the worksheet ahead of the chrome).
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

**[P2] Mouse users get sub-24px targets the touch rules never reach**
- **Why it matters.** B measured, with a mouse at 1280×900: the pin control **16×11**, the quantity field **32×18**, the expression field height **18px**. The 44px minimums are gated on `(any-pointer:coarse)`, so a desktop mouse qualifies for none of them, and WCAG 2.5.8's 24px floor applies to pointers generally. An estimator aims at an 11px-tall dot dozens of times a day.
- **Fix.** Apply a 24px floor unconditionally — row fields to 24px, pin hit area to 24×24 — and keep 44px for coarse pointers.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

## Minor Observations

- The `title` attribute on the pin control stays `"Add to total"` after the pin is on, while `aria-label` flips — a static/dynamic mismatch (B, incidental).
- The phone key row offers `' " × + # @ =` but omits `/`, though nearly every line contains a fraction. Trade `×` for `/`.
- Quick Calc opens without focus in its input, and its `Insert` action is a grey link the same weight as the close `✕`.
- Right-click gives materially different menus depending on which pixel column you hit; `Copy result`, `Insert line below` and `Insert separator below` exist nowhere else in the app.
- `Tally v1.10.0 · browser tab` in Settings → About: "browser tab" is developer-speak for "not installed".
- `DEFAULT STOCK (UNTAGGED)` — an all-caps label with an unexplained parenthetical at the top of the Cut tab.
- Cut tab wraps numbers mid-value in a 270px column (`19 channels · run 139′ 9 15/16″` breaking across three lines), and repeats four identical stick rows where `4 × 94 1/2″ from 96″ stock` would do.
- Default worksheet name `Worksheet` appears four times on the first-run screen.
- Vocabulary drift: Worksheets / PROJECTS / Project sidebar / New worksheet, all for one concept.

## Questions to Consider

1. **Who has held a printout from this app?** The Cut plan is `display:none` on paper at every width, pin radios print as furniture, and an unfinished line prints its placeholder as a line item. If the answer is nobody, the deliverable the tool exists to produce has never been reviewed.
2. **Why does `Auto` exist when it is byte-identical to `Ft+In`?** Seven chips in the most prominent slot, two the same, none able to remove the three unit strings they appear to control. Is the format switcher a feature, or residue from the generic calculator this was built on — the same lineage that put `20% off $19` in Quick Calc?
3. **Five toggles let each person hide a different region. In an office where estimators check each other's takeoffs over a shoulder, whose problem does that solve?** `Everything on` / `Bare calculator` reads like an unresolved argument about what Tally is.
4. **Four section subtotals can be on screen at once and their sum appears nowhere.** Why must an estimator add four fractional-inch numbers by hand in an app whose purpose is adding fractional-inch numbers?
5. **The specificity is all in the Cut tab and the data model; the top 86px is generic.** What would this look like if the purchasing answer were the front of the app rather than the fourth tab?
